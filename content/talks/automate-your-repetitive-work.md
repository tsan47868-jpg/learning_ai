Kenji Nakamura has spent a career making boring work disappear. In this session he shows how to find the tasks worth automating, design workflows that survive real life, and add AI exactly where it helps — without building something fragile you will quietly abandon in a month.

## What You'll Learn

**Pick the Right Task**
Not everything repetitive is worth automating. Kenji shares his filter: frequent, rule-based, reversible, and low-judgment. If a task fails one of those tests, it probably wants a human, a checklist, or no change at all.

**Map Before You Build**
Before touching a tool, you will sketch a workflow as a simple chain: trigger, steps, decisions, outcome. Kenji shows why this ten-minute habit prevents the classic failure of automating the wrong thing very efficiently.

**Where AI Fits**
AI earns its place in the steps that need judgment on messy input — summarizing, classifying, drafting, extracting. Kenji demonstrates each pattern on a realistic example: turning a messy inbox into a prioritized action list.

**Keep a Human in the Loop**
Kenji's rule for first versions: automate the suggestion, not the action. Approval steps, logs, and an off-switch come first; full autonomy is something a workflow earns.

## Who Should Attend

Anyone who repeats the same digital tasks weekly — operations, marketing, admin, support, or solo builders. No coding required for the core patterns.

*"Automate the dull parts, keep the judgement parts, and measure the hours you get back."* — Kenji Nakamura
