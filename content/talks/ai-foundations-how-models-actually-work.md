Marie Dubois opens the learning path with the session everything else builds on: what an AI model really is, what it can do, and where it quietly goes wrong. No math background required — just curiosity and a willingness to unlearn a few things you have read online.

## What You'll Learn

**Models, Not Magic**
Marie explains what happens when a model "answers" a question: it predicts likely text from patterns it saw during training. That single idea resolves most myths — why AI can sound confident while being wrong, and why it never truly "looks things up" unless you build that in.

**Training vs. Using**
You will see the difference between training a model and prompting one, why most learners only ever do the second, and what that means for the answers you get. Analogy: training is writing the textbook; prompting is asking a student a question.

**Where the Limits Are**
Marie walks through the failure modes you will meet in real work — stale knowledge, invented details, brittle reasoning on long tasks — and the habit that catches them: treating the output as a draft, never as a source.

**A Shared Vocabulary**
Tokens, parameters, context windows, hallucination. By the end you will use these words precisely, which turns every later conversation about AI from intimidating to ordinary.

## Who Should Attend

Complete beginners, curious professionals, and anyone who has used a chatbot but never understood what was happening behind it. No technical background needed.

*"You do not need to understand engines to drive — but you should know what is under the hood, and when to check the oil."* — Marie Dubois
